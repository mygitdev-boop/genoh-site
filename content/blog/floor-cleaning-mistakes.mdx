Floors take a beating every single day — footsteps, spills, pet paws, kids dragging toys across them. So when a floor starts looking dull or scratched, it's tempting to blame age or bad luck. More often, the real cause is how the floor gets cleaned. A few small habits, repeated daily, do more damage than years of foot traffic ever could.

## 1. Using too much cleaner "just to be safe"

More concentrate doesn't mean more clean — it usually means more residue. Excess cleaner that isn't fully mopped up dries into a thin, sticky film that actually attracts more dust, making floors look dull faster than if you'd used the correct amount in the first place.

## 2. Skipping the dilution step

Pouring cleaner straight onto the floor, or eyeballing the water ratio instead of measuring it, is one of the most common shortcuts — and one of the easiest to fix. Most floor cleaners, including GENOH, are formulated to work at a specific concentration. Too strong, and you're back to problem #1.

## 3. Mopping with water that's already dirty

If you're doing a large area, that bucket of water is picking up dirt fast. Past a certain point, you're no longer cleaning the floor — you're redistributing yesterday's dirt in a thin layer across today's floor. Changing the water halfway through a big room makes a bigger difference than most people expect.

## 4. Ignoring what the surface actually needs

Marble, tiles, granite and Kota stone don't all behave the same way. Marble is porous and sensitive to acidic cleaners over time; tiles are more forgiving; granite can dull with the wrong pH. Using one aggressive, all-purpose product on every surface in the house is convenient, but it's rarely the gentlest option for each floor.

## 5. Letting spills sit "for later"

A spill that sits for an hour has already started working its way into grout lines and porous stone. The cleaning products used later have to work much harder — and often more aggressively — to undo damage that a 30-second wipe would have prevented entirely.

> The floors that age best usually aren't the ones cleaned the most — they're the ones cleaned correctly.

## 6. Using the same mop head for months

A mop head that's overdue for a wash or replacement doesn't clean — it smears. Bacteria and old residue trapped in the fibres get reapplied to the floor with every pass. If your mop smells even slightly musty, that smell is transferring to your floor too.

## 7. Treating "daily mopping" as the only cleaning step

A quick daily mop is great for surface dust, but it isn't a substitute for an occasional deeper clean with a bit more cleaner concentration on tough spots — under furniture, near entryways, in corners that the mop skips over in a hurry.

None of these fixes require buying anything new — just a small adjustment to routine. The right formula, used the right way, is doing most of the heavy lifting already.
