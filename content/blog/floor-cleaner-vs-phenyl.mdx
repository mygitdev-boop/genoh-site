In a lot of Indian households, "phenyl" has become the generic word for anything you pour into a mopping bucket — the way "Xerox" became shorthand for photocopying. But phenyl and a modern floor cleaner formula are built differently, and that difference shows up in your home.

## What phenyl actually is

Traditional phenyl (phenolic disinfectant) was designed primarily as a strong-smelling disinfectant, often for outdoor areas, drains and heavy-duty spaces — not specifically for daily indoor floor care. It's typically formulated to kill germs through a sharp, often overpowering fragrance, with less attention to how it interacts with delicate floor surfaces or skin during regular handling.

## What a floor cleaner formula does differently

A dedicated floor cleaner — like GENOH's — is built around a non-ionic surfactant base, meaning it's designed to lift dirt and grime effectively while being gentler on the surface it's cleaning and the hands using it. It still carries a disinfectant component, but the formula is balanced for daily indoor use across multiple floor types: tiles, marble, granite, ceramic and stone.

## Smell isn't the same as clean

This is probably the biggest misconception. A strong, sharp fragrance feels like proof of a deep clean, but fragrance intensity and actual cleaning performance are two different things. Some of the strongest-smelling products are covering odour rather than removing dirt at the source — and an overpowering scent can also be a sign of a harsher chemical concentration than necessary for routine mopping.

## Where phenyl still makes sense

To be fair, phenyl has its place — bathrooms with heavy odour issues, outdoor drains, or spaces that need aggressive disinfection more than surface care. It's just not the ideal daily choice for the floors you and your family walk on barefoot every day.

> The right question isn't "which smells stronger" — it's "which is actually formulated for this floor, used this often."

## Our take for daily home use

For everyday mopping across a mixed-surface home, a floor cleaner formulated for that exact job tends to do better — cleaner floors, less residue, a fragrance that fades naturally instead of lingering as a chemical smell, and a gentler experience for anyone doing the mopping by hand. GENOH Floor Cleaner is diluted at 2 capfuls (about 40ml) per 5 litres of water, giving a consistent, predictable result without needing to guess at concentration.

If your current routine is built around phenyl out of habit rather than a specific need, it might be worth trying a dedicated floor cleaner for a week and comparing how your floors — and your hands — feel afterward.
